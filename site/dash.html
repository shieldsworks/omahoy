Aboard Dashplan

AI shed

The plan to make Dash, a 1977 Pacific Seacraft 25 in the Berkeley Marina, an “AI shed” (DHH’s term): local AI running aboard, starting with an 8 GB M1 Mac mini on Omarchy, then AI features in the Omahoy nav apps.

[ plan ] Prices and yields are estimates unless a date is given. This is not an installation drawing, and Omahoy is not for navigation. The install guide covers the software.

What’s on the boat today

  • Dash has no house bank yet. Two car batteries serve as starters, and also run the radio and running lights.
  • An inboard Yanmar YSB8 diesel charges those starter batteries from its alternator while the engine is running.
  • The marina has shore power, but Dash has no inlet. An extension cord runs through the companionway to two trickle chargers.
  • There is no dodger or bimini. Panels can mount on the stern pulpit.

The extension cord through the companionway is a stopgap, and a shock and fire risk in a wet cockpit. A real inlet comes first, before the lithium.

Phases

All prices below are ballpark estimates, not verified quotes, except where a date is given on the shopping list. The sensible DIY total of about $3,200–6,000 covers phases 1–4. Phase 5 and the inverter are extra. Check with the insurer before a lithium retrofit, and follow ABYC E-11 and E-13.

  • Phase 1, shore power $800–1,400

    A 30 A inlet (Marinco or SmartPlug) and a marine shore cord, a Blue Sea 30 A ELCI main, a galvanic isolator, a small AC panel, a Victron Blue Smart IP67 12/25 charger for the house bank, and a maintainer or a 2-bank charger (a Blue Smart IP22 with three outputs is one option) for the starters. This replaces the extension cord.

  • Phase 2, house bank $1,300–2,800, plus $300–600 for wire

    200 Ah of 12 V LiFePO4 as two 100 Ah batteries (Battle Born, Epoch or SOK), mounted low near the centerline, boxed and strapped for a knockdown. A Class T fuse at the house positive, Blue Sea m-Series switches, bus bars, a Victron SmartShunt 500 A (omahoy can read it via BLE Instant Readout with victron-ble, or via VE.Direct USB), a DC panel or SafetyHub, and a fused 100 W USB-C PD outlet for the Starlink Mini and laptops.

  • Phase 3, alternator to house $200–300

    A Victron Orion-Tr Smart 12/12-18 isolated, not the 30 A model. The stock YSB8 alternator is reportedly 35 A and likely gives about 15–25 A at cruise; that figure is from parts listings and owner write-ups, and the label on this engine should be checked, because a 1977 engine may have been re-alternated. Don’t connect lithium directly to the alternator, and don’t use a charging relay.

  • Phase 4, stern-pulpit solar $600–1,100

    Two rigid 100–150 W panels on rail mounts, and two SmartSolar 75/15 controllers, one per panel, so shading from the backstay or sails on one panel doesn’t drag the other down. Estimated yield for 200 W / 300 W: summer 600–800 / 900–1,200 Wh/day; spring and fall 500–700 / 750–1,050 Wh/day; winter 250–400 / 350–600 Wh/day, with Bay fog reducing it. That is enough for the mini 24/7 from spring through fall. Winter leans on shore power.

  • Phase 5, wind instruments

    A masthead wind sensor, for polars. Option A is wireless: the Calypso Ultrasonic Portable (solar, Bluetooth), the easiest fit with the mast up. Option B is wired NMEA 2000 (the Calypso ULP, or a B&G or Garmin transducer, a backbone and a USB gateway), the most reliable, and easiest with the mast down. Add an IMU heel sensor; a specific model is still open. Phase 5 has no complete budget yet: two Calypso unit prices are on the shopping list, and the gateway, mount and IMU are still to be chosen.

  • Optional inverter about $150–300

    A 300–500 W pure-sine inverter (a Victron Phoenix VE.Direct is one option) if the mini must run away from the dock. At the dock, run larger compute such as a Studio or Spark from shore AC.

Connectivity and compute

  • A Starlink Mini, about 25–40 W, on the USB-C PD outlet.
  • Tailscale to reach the boat from anywhere, on the free Personal plan.
  • Local inference on the M1 mini: llama.cpp with Vulkan has run a 3B model at about 18 tok/s. Bigger machines stay on shore power.

Omahoy AI, planned and not built

The math is deterministic. A local model explains the suggestions, offline. None of this is built yet, and Omahoy is not for navigation.

  • A polar learned from omalogbook tracks. True wind from the masthead sensor, or from omawind’s shore stations when that is all there is. Speed through the water from GPS speed over ground, corrected with omatide’s NOAA current predictions, because Bay currents of 2–4 kt would otherwise swamp the polar. Motoring segments are filtered out.
  • Routing from wind, tide and that polar.
  • Sail choice, and reef suggestions learned from /reef log marks plus wind and heel. Thresholds come from Dash’s data, not from a generic racer’s numbers. A published polar for the PS25 is not known here.
  • Power awareness from the Victron data.
  • A local model that explains each suggestion.

Next time at the boat

  • The alternator label: amps, and whether it is the original.
  • Stern pulpit width, and clearance from the backstay and boom.
  • Which locker could hold the house bank, low and near the centerline.
  • DIY versus a professional install.
  • The budget ceiling.
  • Insurer approval for a lithium retrofit.
  • The mast wiring path, or a mount spot for the wind sensor.
